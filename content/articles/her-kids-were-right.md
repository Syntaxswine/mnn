It started, as these things do, at the dinner table.

A mom. Her grown children. A comment about something she'd read online. And then the sound every mother knows: the collective sigh of people who love you and would like you to stop doing the thing.

## The moment everything changed

She did what any of us would do. She said "I'm fine." She said "I've been doing it for years." She said the one that always works: "I raised you, you know."

And they said: "We know. That's why we're asking."

## What the experts won't tell you

Here's the thing the kids have figured out that nobody says out loud. When your kids nag you about your health, they are not trying to run your life. They are scared of a world without you in it. Nagging is just what fear sounds like when it's trying to be polite.

They have also, almost certainly, read the same articles you have. They just read the boring version. The version with the footnotes.

## The 3-step method that changed everything

1. **Let them finish.** The sentence after "but Mom" is the one that matters.
2. **Ask what they actually want you to do.** Usually it's small. Make the appointment. Take the pill. Put the ice down. Call someone.
3. **Take it to the doctor.** Your kids are not doctors (unless they are, in which case, lucky you). The doctor gets the final word. But "my kids are worried about X" is a wonderful sentence to open an appointment with.

> Your kids being right does not mean you were wrong. It means you did your job.

She's fine now, by the way. She made the appointment. The kids have moved on to nagging her about something else, which is how you know a family is healthy.
