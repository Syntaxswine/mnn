It's 2 a.m. You woke up for no reason. And there, on the nightstand, glowing softly, is the object this entire article is about.

You know what it is. You may be reading this on it.

## How it gets you

The phone is not evil. The phone is just very, very good at its job, and its job is to keep you looking at it. A quick check of the time becomes a quick check of the news becomes a quick article about a woman who chewed ice, and now it's 2:40 and your brain is wide awake and a little bit worried about iron.

Bright light in the middle of the night tells your body it's morning. Your body believes it. Your body is very trusting.

## The 'one weird trick' that actually works

Charge it somewhere else. The kitchen. The hallway. Across the room, face down, on silent. If you use it as an alarm clock, a cheap alarm clock costs less than one of those sleep gummies and does not have a news feed.

> If you can't reach it from the bed, you can't read it from the bed.

## The three rules nobody follows

1. **Same bedtime, roughly.** Your body likes a routine even if you don't.
2. **Screen out of arm's reach.** See above.
3. **If you wake up, don't check the time.** Knowing it's 2:47 has never once helped anyone fall back asleep.

## When it's not the phone

Sometimes it is not the phone. If you've been sleeping badly for weeks, if you're snoring loud enough to be mentioned, if you wake up gasping, if you're exhausted no matter how long you sleep, that is a doctor conversation. Sleep problems are something doctors actually see a lot of and can actually do something about. A website cannot. A gummy cannot. Say it at the appointment.

Now put the phone down. We'll be here in the morning.
