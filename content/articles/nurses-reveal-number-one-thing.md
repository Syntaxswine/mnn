Nurses see everything. The 3 a.m. emergencies. The man who "didn't want to bother anyone" about the chest pain. The woman who has been taking two different pills that turned out to be the same pill.

We asked what they wish every single patient would do. The answer was unanimous, immediate, and a little bit tired.

## "Bring the list."

That's it. **A list of everything you take.** Prescriptions, yes, but also the ibuprofen, the sleep aid, the vitamin D, the thing from the health food store, the tea your neighbor swears by. Names and doses.

## Why this one small thing is so big

When you turn up at a doctor's office or, heaven forbid, an emergency room, the first thing they need to know is what's already in you. Not "a little white one" or "the heart one." The name. The dose.

Without the list, they're guessing. With the list, they can see at a glance if two things clash, if something's doubled up, or if the dizziness you came in about is actually a side effect of something you've been taking for years.

## The five-minute method

1. Gather every bottle in the house. Yes, the bathroom cabinet too.
2. Write down the name, the dose, and how often, for each one.
3. Put the paper in your purse. Take a photo with your phone.
4. Every time something changes, update it. Cross things out. It doesn't have to be pretty.
5. Hand it over at every appointment, before they ask.

> A list in your purse is worth more than any supplement in your cabinet.

## The bonus move

Bring a second small list: the questions you've been meaning to ask. The thing you've been searching online about for three weeks. Write it down, because the moment you sit on the paper-covered table your mind will go blank. It happens to everyone. The nurses know.
